Shikhar Shiromani

Hi! I am an ML Engineer at Apple, where I work on improving power and performance across Apple’s operating systems using intelligent algorithms. Before Apple I was at NVIDIA, where I worked on predicting node failures to keep large GPU fleets up, and earlier on low-level, low-latency software for autonomous driving.

Outside that, I work on AI safety and mechanistic interpretability. The question I keep returning to is whether a model’s stated reasoning bears any load. The interesting question is not whether an explanation sounds plausible, but whether it is causally connected to the mechanism that produced the answer, and what our monitoring tools are actually measuring when it is not.

I am a Pivotal Research Fellow, working with Noah Y. Siegel at Google DeepMind on chain-of-thought faithfulness, and a MARS V Research Fellow at Meridian, working with Julian Schulz on steganographic reasoning. Earlier in 2026 I was a SPAR Research Fellow at Kairos, where I worked on attention consistency training.

If you work on faithfulness, monitorability, or evaluation, I would be glad to hear from you.

  1. Benchmarking Pluralistic Alignment Through Persona-Conditioned Behavioral Evaluation
    Archie Chaudhury, Shikhar Shiromani, and Ayushi Mehta
    Pluralistic Alignment Workshop at ICML 2026, Seoul, Jul 2026
    Abs Bib

    We introduce the Pluralistic Alignment Benchmark (PAB), covering 320 scenarios, 8 behavioral-risk categories, and 63 synthetic personas. Evaluating 9 leading LLMs with a multi-judge pipeline reveals substantial persona-conditioned drift in manipulation, concealment, persuasion, and sycophancy.

  2. Visuals Lie, Consistency Speaks: Disentangling Spatial Attention from Reliability in Vision-Language Models
    Logan Mann, Yi Xia, Ajit Saravanan, Ishan Dave, Saadullah Ismail, and 4 more authors
    Workshop on Multimodal Intelligence at ICLR 2026, Rio de Janeiro, Apr 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    A common intuition, which we call the Attention-Confidence Assumption, holds that reliability follows from structural visual perception. We challenge this through the VLM Reliability Probe (VRP), a cross-family study of reliability signals in contemporary VLMs. Spatial attention has near-zero correlation with accuracy, while self-consistency is the dominant predictor of truth. Scaling causal interventions exposes a sharp architectural divergence between late-fusion and early-fusion families.

  3. Where Reliability Lives in Vision-Language Models: A Mechanistic Study of Attention, Hidden States, and Causal Circuits
    Logan Mann, Ajit Saravanan, Ishan Dave, Shikhar Shiromani, Saadullah Ismail, and 2 more authors
    Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop at ICML 2026, Seoul, Jul 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    We instrument three open-weight VLM families with a unified mechanistic pipeline that compares attention structure, generation dynamics, and hidden-state geometry against a single correctness label. Attention structure is a near-zero predictor of correctness, reliability becomes legible later in the computation, and causal neuron-level ablations expose a sharp architectural split with direct monitor-design implications.

  4. Linear Predictability of Attention Heads in Large Language Models
    Khalid Shaikh, Asmit Kumar Singh, Rebecca Christopher Dsouza, and Shikhar Shiromani
    SELVA Workshop at ACL 2026, San Diego (Oral, 1 of 4), Jul 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Oral, one of four selected

    We show that pretrained Transformers exhibit a pervasive inter-head linear structure: the QKV vectors of an attention head can often be reconstructed as a linear combination of a small number of peer heads. This predictability is learned rather than architectural, rising during pretraining. We exploit the redundancy by caching only reference-head KV states and reconstructing the rest on the fly, achieving 2x KV-cache reduction with model-dependent accuracy trade-offs.

  5. ProMoral-Bench: Evaluating Prompting Strategies for Moral Reasoning and Safety in LLMs
    Rohan Subramanian Thomas, Shikhar Shiromani, Abdullah Chaudhry, Ruizhe Li, Vasu Sharma, and 2 more authors
    PersonaLLM/PersonaNLP Workshop at NeurIPS 2025, Mexico City, Dec 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    We introduce ProMoral-Bench, a unified benchmark evaluating 11 prompting paradigms across four LLM families. Using ETHICS, Scruples, WildJailbreak, and our new robustness test ETHICS-Contrast, we measure performance via the Unified Moral Safety Score. Compact, exemplar-guided scaffolds outperform complex multi-stage reasoning at a lower token cost.

  6. COMPASS: Context-Modulated PID Attention Steering System for Hallucination Mitigation
    Kenji Sahay, Snigdha Pandya, Rohan Nagale, Anna Lin, Shikhar Shiromani, and 2 more authors
    AAAI 2026 Workshops, Singapore: LaMAS (Oral), FrontierIR, XAI4Science, TrustAgent, AIR-FM, Jan 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Oral at LaMAS; accepted at five AAAI-26 workshops

    COMPASS is a feedback-driven decoding framework that uses a Context Reliance Score to steer attention heads toward provided evidence during generation. Without retraining or multiple passes, it reduces contextual hallucinations by 2.8-5.8 percentage points across HotpotQA, XSum, HaluEval, and RAGTruth.

  7. ChameleonBench: Quantifying Alignment Faking in Large Language Models
    Archie Chaudhury and Shikhar Shiromani
    The 17th Asian Conference on Machine Learning (Conference Track), PMLR, Nov 2025
    Abs Bib

    Oral presentation

    An 800-prompt benchmark across 8 misbehavior categories with an external judge pipeline for alignment-faking evaluation. Across 6 leading LLMs we observe up to 20% higher harmful response rates in free-form versus test-like settings.
