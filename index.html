Shikhar Shiromani

Machine Learning Engineer at Apple. Independent research on AI safety and interpretability.

I am a Machine Learning Engineer at Apple, where I work on power and performance across Apple’s operating systems.

Separately, in a personal capacity, I study AI safety and interpretability. The question I keep returning to is whether we can trust what a language model says about its own reasoning. When a model explains an answer, that explanation may or may not reflect the computation that produced it. I work on ways to measure that gap.

I am a Pivotal Research Fellow, working with Noah Y. Siegel at Google DeepMind on chain-of-thought faithfulness, and a MARS V Research Fellow at Meridian, working with Julian Schulz on steganographic reasoning. Earlier in 2026 I was a SPAR Fellow at Kairos. Before Apple I spent six years at NVIDIA, across systems, infrastructure, and ML research.

If you work on faithfulness, monitorability, or evaluation, I would be glad to hear from you.

selected publications

  1. ACL-W
    Linear Predictability of Attention Heads in Large Language Models
    Khalid Shaikh, Asmit Kumar Singh, Rebecca Christopher Dsouza, and Shikhar Shiromani
    In SELVA Workshop at ACL 2026, San Diego (Oral, 1 of 4), Jul 2026
    Awarded Abs arXiv Bib

    Oral, one of four selected

    We show that pretrained Transformers exhibit a pervasive inter-head linear structure: the QKV vectors of an attention head can often be reconstructed as a linear combination of a small number of peer heads. This predictability is learned rather than architectural, rising during pretraining. We exploit the redundancy by caching only reference-head KV states and reconstructing the rest on the fly, achieving 2x KV-cache reduction with model-dependent accuracy trade-offs.

  2. AAAI-W
    COMPASS: Context-Modulated PID Attention Steering System for Hallucination Mitigation
    Kenji Sahay, Snigdha Pandya, Rohan Nagale, Anna Lin, Shikhar Shiromani, and 2 more authors
    In AAAI 2026 Workshops, Singapore: LaMAS (Oral), FrontierIR, XAI4Science, TrustAgent, AIR-FM, Jan 2026
    Awarded Abs arXiv Bib

    Oral at LaMAS; accepted at five AAAI-26 workshops

    COMPASS is a feedback-driven decoding framework that uses a Context Reliance Score to steer attention heads toward provided evidence during generation. Without retraining or multiple passes, it reduces contextual hallucinations by 2.8-5.8 percentage points across HotpotQA, XSum, HaluEval, and RAGTruth.

  3. ACML
    ChameleonBench: Quantifying Alignment Faking in Large Language Models
    Archie Chaudhury and Shikhar Shiromani
    In The 17th Asian Conference on Machine Learning (Conference Track), PMLR, Nov 2025
    Awarded Abs Bib

    Oral presentation

    An 800-prompt benchmark across 8 misbehavior categories with an external judge pipeline for alignment-faking evaluation. Across 6 leading LLMs we observe up to 20% higher harmful response rates in free-form versus test-like settings.
